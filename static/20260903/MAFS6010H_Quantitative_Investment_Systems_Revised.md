# MAFS6010H Quantitative Investment Systems: Data, AI, Portfolio Engineering and Execution

Winter 2026-27（Jan 2027）

Instructor: Dr. Don Huang

## Course Description

The objective of this course is to equip students with a practical and
systematic understanding of how mathematical, statistical, and
computational models are transformed into real-world investment
decisions in modern asset management. Rather than viewing quantitative
investing as a collection of isolated techniques, the course adopts an
integrated framework that follows the complete investment decision
chain:

**Information → model → forecast → portfolio → trade → monitoring and
validation.**

A central theme throughout the course is the gap between an idealized
mathematical model and a deployable investment system. Expected returns,
covariance matrices, and machine-learning forecasts are estimated with
error; portfolio solutions may be unstable or excessively sensitive to
model assumptions; and an attractive theoretical strategy may perform
very differently once turnover, liquidity, transaction costs, taxes, and
execution are taken into account. Students will learn not only how to
construct a model, but also how to challenge it, understand when it can
be trusted, and determine whether it can survive real-world
implementation.

The course focuses on the system layer around these models: how
estimated signals and risk models are translated into portfolios, how
uncertainty propagates into investment decisions, and how those
decisions are implemented and validated in practice. It is
differentiated from existing statistics, machine-learning, AI,
market-microstructure, and Python courses by focusing on their
integration into an end-to-end quantitative investment process.

This course is designed for students interested in quantitative finance,
asset management, financial data science, and AI-driven investment
systems. It is suitable for students with backgrounds in mathematics,
statistics, computer science, engineering, or finance.

## Key Topics

-   Point-in-time financial data and prevention of research leakage
-   Data quality, survivorship bias, and look-ahead bias
-   Factor investing and factor risk models
-   Covariance estimation and risk modelling
-   Estimation-aware portfolio construction
-   Robust and constrained portfolio optimization
-   Benchmark-relative portfolio engineering
-   Direct indexing strategies
-   Tax-aware portfolio construction and tax-loss harvesting
-   Multi-period rebalancing under transaction costs and tax
    considerations
-   Financial machine learning methods
-   Large Language Models (LLMs) and AI-generated investment signals
-   AI-assisted quantitative investment workflows
-   Market impact, liquidity, and transaction cost analysis
-   Optimal execution and implementation strategies

The mathematical foundation of the course is closely linked to practical
investment challenges involving estimation uncertainty, regularization
methods, constrained optimization, discrete decision-making, dynamic
portfolio rebalancing, and implementation costs.

## Quantitative Investment System Project

A major component of the course is a Quantitative Investment System
Project. Students will design and develop a complete quantitative
investment process that integrates data management, signal generation,
risk modelling, portfolio construction, implementation, and performance
evaluation.

Students will gain hands-on experience in building an end-to-end
quantitative investment workflow, from financial data processing and
signal research to portfolio construction and performance evaluation.

Assessment will emphasize methodological soundness, robustness,
transparency of assumptions, and practical deployability. Students will
be expected to critically evaluate their own models and demonstrate that
their proposed investment systems can withstand rigorous technical
scrutiny beyond producing attractive historical backtest results.
